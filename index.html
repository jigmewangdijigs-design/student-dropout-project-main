THE PROBLEM

From student records to earlier insight.

“

Research question

Using the 36 UCI predictors, how do four classifiers compare under training-only stratified cross-validation, and how does the selected model perform for Dropout, Enrolled and Graduate students on an untouched holdout set?

Why it matters

Student attrition has academic, social and economic consequences. Predictive analytics can help institutions recognize patterns associated with risk and support more timely, targeted intervention—but predictions should support human decision-making rather than replace it.

01

Compare four classification algorithms against a simple majority-class baseline.

02

Evaluate overall and per-class performance using accuracy, F1 and ROC-AUC.

03

Identify influential academic and administrative signals without claiming causation.

TARGET DISTRIBUTION

The classes are imbalanced: Graduate is the largest class, while Enrolled is the smallest. This is why we report weighted F1 alongside accuracy.

Academic

Admission grade, previous qualification, curricular units, evaluations, approved units and semester grades.

Demographic

Age at enrollment, gender, nationality, marital status, international and displaced status.

Socioeconomic

Scholarship, debtor status, tuition-fee status, parental qualification and occupation.

Macroeconomic

Unemployment rate, inflation rate and GDP.

What the verified code does

01Structure and qualityChecks 4,424 rows, 36 predictors, missing values and exact duplicate rows; both quality counts are zero.
02Target labelsRetains the three outcomes—Dropout, Enrolled and Graduate—as readable string labels.
03Feature preprocessingOne-hot encodes 17 integer-coded categorical fields so arbitrary category codes are not treated as continuous distances.
04Stratified splitCreates 3,539 training records and an untouched 885-record test set with class proportions preserved.
05Fold-safe transformationsFits encoding inside every training fold; continuous inputs are additionally standardized inside the Logistic Regression pipeline.

EVALUATION PLAN

Training-only 5-fold stratified cross-validation

All models use the same training folds. Weighted F1 selects the model; the untouched stratified 20% holdout is then used once for final accuracy, per-class scores, confusion matrix and macro one-vs-rest ROC-AUC.

AccuracyPrecisionRecallWeighted F1Macro F1Confusion MatrixMacro OvR ROC-AUC

Accuracy

The share of all predictions that are correct.

Precision

Of the students predicted as a class, the share actually in that class.

Recall

Of the students truly in a class, the share the model identifies.

Weighted F1

Balances precision and recall, then weights each class by its size.

Confusion matrix

Shows exactly which true classes are confused with which predictions.

Macro OvR ROC-AUC

Measures probability ranking for each class versus the rest, then averages all three classes equally.

WHAT THE RESULTS MEAN

The useful signal is mostly academic—but prediction is not intervention.

All four trained models beat the majority-class baseline. Histogram Gradient Boosting achieved the highest mean training-CV weighted F1. On the untouched holdout, Enrolled was the hardest class: only 65 of 159 cases were identified, while 43 were predicted as Dropout and 51 as Graduate. Its 40.88% recall is much lower than Dropout at 72.18% and Graduate at 88.69%.

The Enrolled group is also the smallest class, and its observed outcomes overlap with both non-completion and graduation patterns. This is a plausible interpretation of the confusion pattern, not a causal finding; class-weighting and timing experiments are needed to test it.

These associations should not be treated as causal explanations or automatic decisions about individual students. A real early-warning system should be validated on local data and used with human oversight.

LIMITATIONS & FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

Useful evidence, with clear boundaries.

01

Class imbalance

Enrolled represents only 17.95% of records and has the weakest recall. Future experiments should compare class weights or fold-safe resampling and report macro F1 alongside weighted F1.

02

Generalizability

The data comes from one Portuguese higher-education institution. Performance may not transfer to other countries, programmes or student populations without external validation.

03

Prediction timing

Strong predictors include first- and second-semester performance, so this is not purely a pre-enrolment model. A future comparison should test enrolment-only, first-semester and second-semester feature sets.

04

Responsible improvement

Future work should use nested tuning when optimizing models, evaluate probability calibration and fairness, and test intervention value with human oversight rather than treating predictions as decisions.
